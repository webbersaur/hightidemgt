Government and Municipal IT Asset Disposition in Connecticut

ITAD, data destruction, and e-waste recycling for Connecticut towns, boards of education, police and fire departments, libraries, and public authorities. Records-retention aware and built for public procurement.

Public sector IT disposal has a problem the private sector does not: almost everything about it is eventually visible. The purchase goes through procurement, the disposal shows up in the audit, the surplus value shows up in the budget, and if something goes wrong it shows up in the minutes and the local paper. High Tide Commodities Management provides IT asset disposition and data destruction for Connecticut municipalities, school districts, and public agencies, with paperwork built for that reality.

What Makes Public Sector ITAD Different

Three constraints separate a town hall refresh from a commercial one:

Public records obligations. Connecticut municipalities operate under records retention schedules issued by the Office of the Public Records Administrator at the Connecticut State Library, and disposing of records generally requires an approved disposition authorization rather than a unilateral decision by the department holding them. Equipment that stored those records has to be reconciled against retention before it is destroyed. Your town clerk or records management liaison is the authority on what has been released, and we work from their sign-off rather than around it.

Overlapping federal frameworks. A single town can be subject to several at once. Police departments handle CJIS-scoped media. Boards of education handle FERPA-protected student data. Any agency receiving federal tax information falls under IRS Publication 1075. Assessor and tax collector systems hold personally identifiable information on every property owner in town. These do not share one handling standard, so scoping matters.

Procurement and transparency. The engagement has to survive a bid file, a purchase order, and an audit. That means itemized written quotes, a W-9, a certificate of insurance, invoicing that matches the PO, and a disposal record specific enough that an auditor can trace an asset tag from the fixed-asset register to a destruction certificate.

School District Refreshes

A Connecticut district retiring a one-to-one fleet is handling volume that town hall never sees: hundreds or thousands of devices, often on a compressed summer timeline, with student data on every one of them. Two things go wrong most often. The first is assuming a deprovisioned Chromebook is a clean Chromebook, which is a device management state, not a data destruction state. The second is losing the count, which turns into an inventory discrepancy the business office has to explain in September.

We inventory district fleets by serial number against your asset list, return a reconciliation you can hand to the business office, remarket what still has value with proceeds credited back to the district, and destroy or recycle the rest. Summer window projects should be scoped in spring, because everyone else's window is the same week as yours.

Law Enforcement and CJIS-Scoped Media

Police department media is normally scoped for on-site destruction under department escort, so drives are removed and destroyed inside the building and nothing data-bearing leaves intact. This is the simplest posture to defend and the one most departments prefer. Agencies should confirm handling against the current CJIS Security Policy media sanitization requirements and any direction from their state CJIS Systems Officer, since those requirements are updated periodically and the department, not the vendor, owns the compliance determination.

Value Recovery Returns to the Budget

Surplus municipal IT is not usually worthless. Recent workstations, network gear, and district laptops in serviceable condition carry real resale value, and under a revenue-share arrangement that value comes back to the town or district instead of to the vendor. For a public entity this changes the conversation entirely: a disposal line item becomes an offset, and the settlement is itemized enough to defend if someone asks how the number was reached. Frequently Asked Questions

Can a Connecticut municipality just throw out old computers?

No. Connecticut's electronics recycling law keeps covered electronics out of the waste stream, and equipment that stored public records carries separate retention obligations under schedules issued by the Office of the Public Records Administrator. Check with your town clerk before releasing records-bearing equipment.

Do you provide written quotes we can use in a bid or council packet?

Yes, itemized and suitable for a bid file or requisition, with a W-9 and certificate of insurance on request and invoicing against your PO.

How is law enforcement media handled?

Typically on-site under department escort, so nothing data-bearing leaves the building intact. Agencies should confirm the method against the current CJIS Security Policy and their state CJIS Systems Officer's direction.

What happens to Chromebooks and tablets from a school district?

Inventoried by serial against your asset list, remarketed where value remains with proceeds returned to the district, and destroyed or recycled otherwise. Devices are treated as data-bearing regardless of deprovisioning status.

Does value recovery come back to the town?

Yes, through an itemized revenue-share settlement, so surplus IT offsets disposal cost instead of adding to it.
